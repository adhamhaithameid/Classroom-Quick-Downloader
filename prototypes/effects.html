A1 · Water fill — single-download pill

The content-script pill, in its real light-surface context

On Classroom pages the pill sits on light gray. Idle is outline blue; clicking runs indeterminate slosh (while the file count is unknown, ~0.8s) then a determinate rise with a dual wave crest, then a green settle. Timing follows the research rule: under 1s no loader, 1–10s indeterminate, over 10s determinate — so determinate fill starts the moment the first file reports.

B · Star shimmer — Buy-Me-a-Coffee

Attention by sparkle, not by pulse

Five four-point stars on staggered 4.8s cycles, each visible only ~25% of the time, plus one sheen pass every 6.5s. Nothing loops fast, nothing scales past the button, and the cup icon never animates — the guidance is uniform: low opacity, transform-only, mostly idle.

Standard motion — live
Stars: 5 × 4.8s, ≤25% duty cycle · sheen: one pass / 6.5s · decorative only (aria-hidden).

Contrast is unaffected: stars live outside the text area at ≤90% peak opacity and the sheen is a 55% white pass over #FFDD00 — the label (700 weight, #241C00 on #FFDD00 ≈ 13.3:1) stays readable throughout.

Reduced-motion fallback — frozen
Stars hold at a static 30% opacity, sheen parked off-canvas. Still noticeably "special", zero movement — motion off doesn't mean emphasis off.

One rule, everywhere: @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) and the in-app .reduce-motion class produce the identical frozen state, so the setting is testable without changing the OS.

Per the ground rules GIF was rejected without a size report because: waves need per-frame alpha blending (GIF: 256 colors + banding on gradients), the tank fill must track arbitrary progress values live (GIF is fixed timeline), and reduced-motion fallbacks must be CSS-gated (GIF cannot respond to a media query or a setting).
