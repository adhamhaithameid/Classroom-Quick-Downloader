01 · Privacy / data-use disclosure

        REQUIRED · store + dashboard
        RECOMMENDED · in-product
      

      
Where your files and data go — ambient line vs one-tap ledger

      
The store listing carries the full policy. In the popup, the user should learn the three facts without leaving: what is read, what stays on the device, what is sent. Two ways to carry that.

      


        

          
Treatment 1RECOMMENDED

          
The quiet line — always visible, zero interaction

          
The idea: the facts live in the footer next to the approved affiliation notice. One short sentence per fact. No tap, no panel, nothing to discover.

          

            
Popup · footer

            

              
              Privacy policy
              Not affiliated with Google. Downloads belong to their owners — follow your school's policies.
              Downloads run in your browser. Your history stays on this device. Nothing is sent to the developer.
            

          

          
Information architecture: ambient disclosure. The line is always present, in the same glance as the approved notice. Options repeats it verbatim under About. Cost: two footer lines.

          
Keeps round 1's rule: concrete nouns only — pages, settings, history, nothing. The full policy stays a link, never inlined.

        


        

          
Treatment 2RECOMMENDED

          
The reassurance ledger — one tap, three directions

          
The idea: the footer stays clean. A "Privacy & data" pill expands a small ledger in place: Reads / Keeps on this device / Sends. Users who want detail get structure, not a wall of text.

02 · Permissions explainer

        REQUIRED · dashboard justification
        RECOMMENDED · in-product
      

      
Why each permission exists — one front-loaded sheet vs a standing reference

      
The dashboard justification is required; the in-product explainer is recommended. Chrome's own prompt is coarse ("Read and change your data on classroom.google.com"), so the product closes the gap. Two placements, two moments.

      


        

          
Treatment 1RECOMMENDED

          
The first-run sheet — explained once, at the moment of attention

          
The idea: a small sheet in the popup on first run names all three permissions in plain words, then never again. Front-load the "why" once; keep every later session clean.

Information architecture: episodic. One interruption at install, then silence. The same strings paste into the dashboard justifications.

          
Keeps round 1's rule: verb-first lines; identity scoped to the beta, matching the registry behavior (isApiConfigured() gates v3).

        


        

          
Treatment 2RECOMMENDED

          
The standing reference — Options rows that expand on tap

          
The idea: no first-run interruption at all. Options carries a permanent "Permissions" section; each row expands to the plain-language reason plus the exact mechanism, in faint type. Trust through specificity, on demand.

03 · API (beta) risk warning

        RECOMMENDED · transparency
      

      
Heads-up at the selector — a calm caption vs an explicit confirm

      
No store rule demands a beta warning, but the beta changes the trust model: a Google sign-in scope is added. Stillwater reframes the warning as a note, not an alarm. The question is how active the moment should be.

      


        

          
Treatment 1RECOMMENDED

          
The calm caption — a note where the eye already is

          
The idea: selecting API β reveals a quiet note under the selector. It names what changes and promises the way back, in body-gray rather than warning-yellow. It leaves when the engine changes.

          

            
Popup · engine selector (live)

            

              
Engine

              

                
                
                
              

              

                
                
API (beta) is new. It signs you in with Google and lists your files through Google's official API. It can be slower to start, and it can break when Google changes. Legacy and New stay one click away.

              

            

          

          
Click API β to show the caption; Legacy or New hides it.

          
Information architecture: passive and contextual. Zero friction; the note lives exactly where the choice happens and never blocks it.

          
Tone shift from round 1: the yellow warning card becomes a body-gray note — Stillwater keeps the facts (experimental, may break, way back) and drops the alarm.

        


        

          
Treatment 2RECOMMENDED

          
The confirm — one explicit "try it" with the way back shown first

          
The idea: the first API β pick opens a small card that states the three facts as choices, and the user confirms. After that first confirm, the calm caption from Treatment 1 takes over. One active moment, then quiet.

04 · Copyright / ToS reminder for downloaded materials

        RECOMMENDED · strongly advised
      

      
Responsible use — one gate before the first big run vs an echo that never interrupts

      
A downloader is a neutral tool, but classroom-wide scale is where overzealous use begins. Round 1 prototyped a checkbox dialog. Round 2 asks a sharper question: interrupt once, or never interrupt and carry the reminder in the action itself?

      


        

          
Treatment 1RECOMMENDED

          
The one-time gate — a short word before the first full download

          
The idea: before the first classroom-wide run, a small dialog states the duty in two sentences and asks for one specific check. Shown once per profile. Every later run stays silent.

          

            
Popup · before first classroom-wide download (live)

            

              

                

                  
One quick word before your first full download

                  
Materials in a classroom belong to the people who made them — your teachers and school.

                  
Keep the files for your own study, and follow your school's rules.

                  
                  

                    
                    
                  

                

              

            

            
Acknowledged once — stored on this device. 

          

          
The check unlocks Download. "Not now" means no download.

          
Information architecture: interrupt once, then silence. The specific check ("for my own study") replaces a hollow "I agree". Afterward, the approved footer notice carries the echo.

          
Keeps round 1's rule: dismissal without the check blocks the run; the acknowledgment is a flat key (e.g. ipReminderAcknowledged).

        


        

          
Treatment 2RECOMMENDED

          
The standing echo — context in the action, zero interruptions

          
The idea: no dialog, ever. The reminder lives in the same glance as the action: the button's sub-line says who the files are for, and the success state closes the loop. Options About repeats the line.

          

            
Popup · classroom action (static states)

            

              

                

                  
Idle

                  

                    
                      
                      Download Classroom24 files across 4 posts · for your own study
                    
                  

                

                

                  
Success

                  

                    
                      
                      Done — 24 filesIn your Downloads folder · files belong to their owners
                    
                  

                

              

            

          

          
Static cells — the full water-fill motion lives in popup-direction-a.html.

          
Information architecture: ambient. The reminder is inseparable from the action, so it cannot be skipped and never interrupts. Repetition does the work the dialog did.

          
Same legal ground as round 1: no facilitation of infringement; naming the duty holder ("their owners") on the two highest-attention surfaces.

05 · Limited Use disclosure

        REQUIRED · store + dashboard
        RECOMMENDED · in-product echo
      

      
The API affirmation — the terse legal pair vs an answered-questions block

      
Because the beta uses Google APIs, the store requires the exact Limited Use sentence in the dashboard and the privacy policy. The in-product echo is recommended. Both treatments keep the sentence verbatim; they differ in how much human framing surrounds it.

      


        

          
Treatment 1RECOMMENDED

          
The two-line block — plain first, legal second, nothing else

          
The idea: the minimum honest echo. One plain sentence a student understands, then the exact policy sentence, smaller and dimmer. Reviewers find their sentence; users read theirs.

          

            
Options · API section

            

              
API (beta) — data use

              
The beta only lists and downloads files from classrooms you are enrolled in.

              
The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

            

          

          
Information architecture: terse. Two lines, fixed order, never merged into one paragraph. The sentence stays verbatim for review.

          
Keeps round 1's rule: the exact sentence appears in the dashboard disclosure and the privacy policy; this echo costs two lines and preempts review questions.

        


        

          
Treatment 2RECOMMENDED

          
The answered-questions block — the why, then the legal line as a footnote

          
The idea: the affirmation arrives with receipts. Three questions cover read / kept / shared, including that the sign-in token is used only when the user starts a beta download. The exact sentence closes the block as a footnote.

          

            
Options · API section, expanded disclosure

            

              
How the beta handles your data

              
What does it read?Titles and download links for files in classrooms you are enrolled in.

              
What stays on this device?Your sign-in token and your download history. The token is used only when you start a beta download.

              
Who gets a copy?No one. Nothing is sent to the developer.

              
The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

            

          

          
Information architecture: reassurance-shaped. Each line answers one user question; the legal sentence remains verbatim, visually a footnote, for reviewers.

          
Store-side anchor (REQUIRED): the dashboard affirmation and the privacy policy carry the legal weight; this block keeps the product consistent with them.
